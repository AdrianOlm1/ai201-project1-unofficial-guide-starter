Reviews About Campus Life

When I first got into UC Santa Cruz from, I wasn’t proud to attend UC Santa Cruz, everyone from my high school cared about rankings, and UCSC was seen as “third-tier.” But after spending time there, I realized how special it is. The campus is breathtaking built inside a redwood forest with ocean views that feel unreal. Every walk to class feels peaceful and grounding. The dining halls are surprisingly great (RCC’s my favorite), and professors are chill, supportive, and genuinely care. During my second year I lost on campus housing and moved to Hilltop, which turned out amazing I brought my car, explored beaches, trails, and the city. Driving back along Highway 1 always felt like returning to paradise. Santa Cruz itself is creative, laid back, and full of kind people. It taught me to slow down, be independent, and enjoy learning. UCSC may not top rankings, but it offers something far more valuable, a calm, authentic college experience that stays with you forever.

Imo, UCSC may not be the school for you if: you're an athlete of any kind (there's 1 gym on campus which holds <200 people), you love being part of a social scene (campus is intentionally fragmented/isolated; there's no quad and many dorms have no common rooms), you want to learn about diverse perspectives and views (people can be very close-minded and unwilling to stray from this very specific brand of white liberalism- you're expected to agree completely or at least pretend, and I say that as a liberal), you want to have fun off-campus (it's difficult to get off campus without a car- the transit system is a topic in itself, and besides the beach town is seedy and quiet). I went into UCSC with so much enthusiasm and excitement, and I've been consistently disappointed since day one. Campus is overrated imo. In short: you want a classic UC high-energy college experience, you get disinterested misanthropes and weed. Two stars are for amazing professors.

Love it here beautiful campus great amenities, there is a serious lack of housing most students have to live off campus after they’re first year , lots of clubs, not a party school it’s ur looking for frat life this isn’t the school for you. It’s a school where you have to find your people and those are the best parties you’ll go to. The school gym is not sufficient for the amount of students it has by far. But the students are really nice chill people. The professor are typical professors so are more engaging than others they all very organized. The biggest school event of the year is 4/20. In the town itself there is stuff to do but it’s a small town so it can get dull fast. The school does have any on campus weekly activities for fun, mental health and building community like painting, karaoke, bbqs etc. They have loop buses that drive around the school to get students to the different places on campus

The campus is very large and hilly, and there is a bus that takes you around campus because some classes may over a 20 minute walk away. There are long wait times for the bus and there are many electric bikes on campus-but sometimes they don't work very well. One large inconvenience about the school is there are no grocery stores on or near to campus, so if you don't have a car you have to take the bus off campus to get groceries and then bus back. Despite all this, the campus is extremely beautiful with nature everywhere and is built inside part of a forest, and also has a field with an ocean view. If you love nature and want to study in a forest, with bridges connecting classrooms, this is the school for you. The academics are not the best out of the UC's, but there are still many very good programs. The diversity can be improved, and the food in the dining halls are known to be much better than the other UC schools. Campus life is overall good, but most parties get shut down early.
